What it does.

Pulse

Heart rate from the fingertip. It reads through the rear camera with the flash on. About thirty seconds, with the live wave for the whole read — not a short preview behind a paywall.

Beat spacing (HRV)

The gap between beats, shown as a number and its trend. No score.

A personal normal

After seven mornings of measuring, each morning's pulse against its own normal range — above or below those previous mornings.

How it works.

  1. 1

    Finger on the rear camera, covering the lens next to the flash.

  2. 2

    Hold still. The wave appears once the finger is seen — never before. No wave appears without a fingertip.

  3. 3

    About thirty seconds later: pulse and beat spacing, with the readings saved on the phone.

When.

Same time, seated, still. In the morning, after waking and before coffee — the resting pulse the way runners have measured it since stopwatches, not a watch's average of the day.

Takes a minute. Any other time of day is a spot check, and it says so.

Fair questions.

Is a camera pulse real?
Open the phone's own camera in video, flash on, finger on the lens: the pulse is visible. Then count a wrist pulse for a minute and compare.
What if the finger slips?
A pulse that isn't there is refused — it won't be reported. A read taken while the heart is still settling is refused too, with the reason.
A weekly subscription?
The price is on this page. Cancel is in the app.
Does it judge the number?
A number and its trend. No AI assistants, no ready-made plans, no verdict on a single reading. Not a medical device.
Blood pressure or oxygen?
No. A phone camera can't, and this one doesn't pretend to.
